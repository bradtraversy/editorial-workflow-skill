# Research Contract

Use this contract for research planning, execution, synthesis, and source verification.

## Assignment inputs

Every research assignment receives:

- Approved brief
- Content mode and research depth
- Narrow research question
- Intended reader
- Known relevant sources
- Time and freshness boundary
- Exclusions and sensitive boundaries
- Exact output artifact or response shape

Give researchers only the context needed for their track.

## Research tracks

### Search and intent

Determine likely reader intent, current result types, recurring questions, necessary coverage, and gaps in existing material. Search results describe the landscape but do not establish truth.

### Primary and authoritative sources

Collect official documentation, original announcements, standards, specifications, primary research, direct datasets, and well-supported specialist analysis when primary material is insufficient.

### Audience and community

Collect recurring questions, confusing terminology, objections, frustrations, and unusual use cases. Treat community material as audience evidence, not proof of a broad claim.

### Firsthand experience

Search only author-approved notes, captures, transcripts, project records, git history, code, tests, existing public work, and exact current-conversation language.

Return verified events, short exact quotes, results, constraints, tradeoffs, failed approaches, bounded inferences, and focused questions for missing details.

### Technical investigation

When practical, reproduce instructions, run examples, inspect current source, compare documented and observed behavior, and record the environment and date.

Do not change production systems or external state merely to collect evidence.

### Counterarguments

Find strong competing explanations, conditions where a recommendation fails, costs, tradeoffs, uncertainty, and credible disagreement. Do not manufacture false balance.

## Search-led research profile

For search-led work, organize the dossier as:

1. Executive summary
2. Search intent and result landscape
3. High-value reader questions
4. Source-backed findings by subtopic
5. Gaps and original-value opportunities
6. Content enrichment opportunities
7. Source appendix

Do not produce the final heading structure. The outline architect owns structure after angle approval.

## Source policy

Prefer sources in this order and adjust for the claim:

1. Direct evidence or primary source
2. Official documentation or standard
3. Peer-reviewed or established research
4. Reputable specialist publication
5. Credible expert commentary
6. Community account
7. Search summary or unsourced aggregation

Verify current products, pricing, policies, people, software behavior, and recommendations at research time. Record the check date for changeable claims.

Use short exact quotations only when wording matters. Preserve the speaker, date, and timestamp when relevant. Never reconstruct a quote.

When sources conflict, record both claims, compare quality, dates, scope, and definitions, resolve only when evidence permits, and otherwise preserve the conflict.

## Research dossier

Produce:

- Executive summary
- Reader and intent findings
- Findings by research question with claim IDs
- Firsthand advantage
- Contradictions and unknowns
- Focused questions for the author
- Three to five evidence-supported angle options
- Enrichment opportunities that improve understanding or credibility
- Source appendix grouped by role

## Failure conditions

Stop and report when:

- The central claim cannot be verified.
- Sources are too weak or contradictory for the intended thesis.
- The angle depends on invented personal experience.
- A current claim cannot be checked with available tools.
- Research requires unauthorized contact or external action.
- Sensitive material cannot be separated safely from the public story.
